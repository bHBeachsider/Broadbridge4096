# Broadbridge Engineering Discovery — questionnaire v1 draft

Date: 27 September 2026
Status: proposed question copy, not a live form or a completed expert decision.
Design: [capture and feedback workflow](../superpowers/specs/2026-09-27-expert-discovery-design.md).

## Introduction shown to participants

We are exploring where AI could help experienced engineers and their teams. Your input will shape the work we choose, the evidence we collect and how we judge the results.

Please describe what matters in your work, including problems we have not suggested. You may type, speak when recording is enabled, or contribute through a facilitated interview. "Unknown", "not applicable" and "I disagree" are useful answers. A short contribution is welcome; you can return later.

Do not include client-identifying or restricted project information unless you are authorized to provide it. Naming material that might be available is enough at this stage. Participation, recording and model-training permissions are separate choices.

The first pass can stop after A and B. The remaining sections are optional prompts for a chosen task or follow-up conversation.

## A. Your experience and context

A01. **Which engineering work do you know best?** Give your role, areas of experience and the facilities, equipment or project stages you work with. Also identify areas where you would want another specialist.

A02. **Who would you like this work to help?** Examples can include yourself, junior engineers, plant operators, engineering managers, owners or customers. Describe their situation in your own words.

A03. **How may we use this contribution?** Record participation/analysis agreement, attributed quotation preference, recording/transcription choice where applicable, and whether we may contact you again. These choices do not grant model-training permission or ownership of employer/client records.

UI: concise participation summary and separate affirmative controls; record wording/version. No recording starts automatically.

## B. Open discovery — before showing our suggested topics

B01. **Think about a recent working week or project. What was harder, slower or more frustrating than it should have been?** Describe the actual job and its trigger.

B02. **If a capable assistant could prepare three pieces of work for you, what would you choose?** Put the most valuable first. What should you still do yourself?

B03. **What important problem have we probably failed to ask about?** Include neglected tasks, unusual combinations of conditions, problems people work around, or things nobody wants to raise.

UI: repeatable issue/task cards; each can be typed or linked to spoken evidence. Free responses precede the suggested list. No minimum word count.

## C. Make a priority task concrete

Repeat this section for up to three initial priorities; additional proposals may be saved without completing every field.

C01. **Describe one job precisely.** "Given [inputs], help [person] produce [deliverable] so they can [decision/action]."

C02. **When does it occur, and what is the current effort or consequence?** Choose an approximate frequency or unknown. Separately describe time/cost, operational impact and the consequence of a wrong answer; an estimate is sufficient.

C03. **What information do you need to do this responsibly?** Which readings, documents, context, units, dates and assumptions matter? Which are commonly missing or misleading?

C04. **What would a useful answer look like?** Name the required evidence, calculations, format and caveats. What should the assistant ask or decline to conclude when information is insufficient?

C05. **What plausible answer would be seriously wrong?** Explain why and what signal an experienced engineer would notice. List acceptable tolerances only where you can justify them.

C06. **Who would use and review the result?** Who experiences the problem, who decides to buy a solution, and how often would they need it? "I don't know" is an acceptable answer.

C07. **What supporting material could be available?** Nominate a report, case, drawing, datasheet, calculation, teaching note or resolved discussion. Identify its likely owner and format without attaching restricted contents.

## D. Rare, surprising or unresolved problems — optional deeper account

D01. **Tell us about a problem where the obvious explanation or usual rule failed.** Mark it as an observed event, reconstructed recollection or hypothetical concern. What made it unusual?

D02. **What was known at the time?** Describe symptoms, operating conditions, available measurements, their reliability and what was missing. Keep later knowledge separate.

D03. **What did people initially believe, and why?** Which observation was overtrusted, dismissed or misinterpreted? What competing explanations were considered?

D04. **What changed the investigation?** If resolved, describe the discriminating observation/calculation, eventual explanation and evidence for the outcome. If unresolved, state what could distinguish the remaining possibilities.

D05. **Where does this lesson stop applying?** Which fluids, equipment, operating regimes or missing assumptions could reverse the conclusion? What additional specialist would you consult?

D06. **Why should we preserve this case even if it is rare?** Describe its potential consequence or the gap it reveals. We will not remove it merely because few people mention it.

UI: unanswered resolution remains unresolved. Original narrative and attribution persist. A later "Nominate as a case" action creates a draft for review, never an automatic signed training record.

## E. Compare possible directions — after open responses

E01. **Which of these would you investigate first, later or not at all?** Include a reason and add anything missing.

Suggested directions:
- Troubleshooting briefs and discriminating follow-up questions.
- Checking calculations, assumptions, units and measurement basis.
- Comparing project documents and equipment data for missing/conflicting information.
- Teaching junior engineers through reviewed cases.
- Finding relevant prior knowledge and explaining its limits.
- CAD/drawing-linked review and equipment/document navigation.
- Facility/geospatial research and potential project investigation.
- Emissions/MRV evidence preparation.
- A new direction proposed by you.

These are product directions, not a list of approved engineering capabilities.

E02. **For your preferred direction, which tools and formats are already in use?** If CAD, specify the package, versions/exports and the comparison you need. If mapping/imagery, name the actual decision and required accuracy. If MRV, identify the reporting method, evidence and accepting specialist.

E03. **What would make the first demonstration worth your time?** Suggest one small input package, useful output and measurable success/failure criterion. Explain what would make you stop using it.

## F. Experts, disagreement and next steps

F01. **Where would another expert add the most value?** Which specialist and which task? For Bill: how might Norm contribute initially, and what deeper role might be worthwhile if he is interested?

F02. **What do you disagree with or remain uncertain about?** You can challenge the questionnaire, its proposed categories, a group conclusion or the idea that AI would help at all.

F03. **What should we investigate next, and may we follow up?** Choose the best next case, question or demonstration. Confirm preferred contact separately from any contribution/use permission.

## Repeat feedback round

Ask after showing an identifiable, versioned result; preserve the original response.

R01. What output/version did you review, and for which task?
R02. What was useful, incorrect, missing or unsupported? Link a specific passage, source or finding.
R03. Did it miss an important exception or make a dangerous assumption?
R04. How much review/correction did it require? Would it change your workflow?
R05. What new problem or follow-up test has this revealed?
R06. Does this change your earlier priorities? Record the new ranking and why.

Participants' feedback is evidence to investigate. An appropriate technical reviewer separately determines whether a reported engineering defect is confirmed.

## Spoken-answer review card

For each supplied transcript or recording, show:
- Session, contributor or unresolved speaker, question/version and source.
- Original passage with timestamps if they actually exist.
- Proposed structured answer and any new issues suggested from it.
- Flagged uncertain terms, numbers, units and negations.
- Actions: correct attribution/text; link to another question; add a new issue; leave unresolved; confirm interpretation.

Record who performed each action. A facilitator's confirmation must not be labeled as the participant's confirmation. Preserve unmatched passages for review.

## Focus-group facilitation

1. Collect brief independent answers to B01-B03 before presenting the suggested topics.
2. Discuss concrete jobs and unusual exceptions; do not require consensus.
3. Attribute statements carefully and allow corrections. An unattributed comment remains unattributed.
4. Capture a group summary separately, including dissent and unresolved items.
5. Ask participants to check their attributed answers and priority changes.
6. Produce a decision log with owner, supporting evidence and a next experiment; do not label a vote as engineering validation.

## Expected output for the SLM and commercial plan

Each selected task becomes: intended user + input boundary + output schema + evidence requirements + acceptance/failure tests + reviewer + eligible source/case nominations.

Track recurring tasks, rare/high-consequence issues and disagreements separately. Route approved engineering content through the existing rights/review/family-split workflow. Market preferences, raw audio and unconfirmed machine summaries do not enter model training automatically.
