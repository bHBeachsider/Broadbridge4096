# Bill: choose the priorities, then contribute a case

Your priorities should determine what we teach and test first. Our proposed
topics are suggestions. You can add an area, remove one, or recommend a different
specialist. You do not need to approve a preselected curriculum.

## First: tell us what matters

Open **A · Your workflow**. Complete the questions that are useful now; use **A8**
for a ranked list of the engineering areas and tasks you want an assistant to
handle. A8 is free text and saves with your workflow answers. Its existing label
asks which work you would delegate and which you would keep; add the priorities
below that answer. No new form, database field or initial call is required.

For each of your top three, please give:

- **Area and unit/service:** what kind of engineering work?
- **Specific task:** what question or deliverable should the assistant handle?
- **Importance:** how often does it arise, what takes time, and what is the
  consequence of getting it wrong?
- **Evidence available:** a case, document, drawing, calculation or measured outcome.
- **A useful result:** what would make you accept the answer or brief?
- **Limits:** what must the assistant leave to a person or another specialist?

Possible areas include distillation/vacuum, heat transfer and fired equipment,
pumps/compressors, steam/utilities, measurements/controls, gas processing, and
design/revamp/commissioning review. They are not ranked or approved. Please add
the areas that matter in your work, including ones absent from this list. The
distillation wording elsewhere in Part A does not limit your suggestions.

Use A6 for common wrong diagnoses, A7 for unacceptable behavior, and A9 for
references or archives that might be available. An initial nomination is enough;
Brad handles permission and storage decisions before collecting private files.

If preferred, use the [priority worksheet](templates/EXPERT_PRIORITY_INPUT.md)
or send Brad the same information privately. Completed answers belong in the
capture/private workflow, not in the public code repository.

## Then: add one representative case

Choose **+ New case**, ideally for a problem in one of your priority areas. Start
with a case you know well. You can contribute as many as you choose.

Record what was known at the time separately from what you learned afterward.
Include missing or distrusted information, alternative explanations, the turning
point and the measured outcome. Add test questions and reference answers where
possible. Unknowns are useful; do not invent observations to fill gaps.

Before sign-off, choose training, testing_only or reference_only deliberately;
the page defaults to training. Signing attests to the record. It does not bypass
source rights, independent review of authored training answers, or dataset-release
approval. Wait for the saved status before leaving the page.

## How your priorities will be used

Brad records the selected scope and resolves source rights and resource needs.
You review the resulting task definitions, required evidence, allowed synthetic
variations and failure criteria within your competence. A new specialist is
named where needed. Only then do selected recipes move from draft to an approved
generation plan. Each generated answer still needs its own review.

The current fabricated pump and exchanger examples test software behavior only.
They do not settle the engineering curriculum or carry your endorsement.

## Public-document scoring is a different activity

The existing scoring packet helps identify helper-model weaknesses; its sources
stay evaluation-only. Your choice of important areas is separate from scoring
that packet: a good answer to an irrelevant problem is not a useful curriculum.
