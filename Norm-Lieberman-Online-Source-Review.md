## Private work papers and emails

No verified public archive of Norm's private email or consulting work papers was found in this search. That does not establish that no such archive exists. Request a curated export from the expert or authorized custodian, if available:

- Complete technical email threads, including the initial question, attachments, follow-up observations, revised diagnosis, intervention and final result.
- Site-visit and troubleshooting reports, marked-up flowsheets, inspection notes, photographs and measurement logs.
- Hand calculations, spreadsheets, equipment curves and comparison of predicted with measured performance.
- Draft papers, presentation slides, seminar workbooks, instructor notes and corrections.
- Unsuccessful interventions, abandoned hypotheses, unresolved cases and later lessons.

For emails, retain chronology and distinguish what was known at the time from hindsight. Preserve relevant technical context while removing unrelated personal content and unnecessary identifiers. Remove repeated signatures and duplicate quoted messages only after preserving a controlled original. Record who supplied each message, attachment and customer permission; Norm's involvement alone does not establish rights to employer, client, coauthor or correspondent material. Anonymization alone does not resolve contractual confidentiality.

For work papers, preserve units, instrument location, date, operating regime, assumption changes, handwritten annotations and final outcome. Require an expert to explain ambiguous shorthand. Mark reconstructed examples and recollections so they cannot be confused with measured plant records.

## Permissions to specify

For each asset, record permission separately for: controlled storage and OCR/transcription; indexing and embeddings; customer-facing retrieval and permitted excerpts; supervised training and synthetic example generation; model evaluation; hosting and continued use of trained weights/adapters; and distribution or sublicensing, if intended. A retrieval grant should not automatically enable fine-tuning or model redistribution.

Identify the actual rightsholder and any coauthor, customer, employer, publisher, event or third-party illustration interests. Elizabeth Lieberman coauthored several identified works; the Hydrocarbon Processing ejector article also names R. Cardoso. Treat these as rights inquiries, not assumptions that every named party controls every use.

Specify attribution, customer scope, security, approved processors, retention, corrections and termination. Resolve treatment of existing trained models at contract end before training: deleting a source file does not remove its influence from model weights. Permission to use Norm's name or imply endorsement is a separate decision.

## Incorporation into the Broadbridge SLM

| Material | Proposed route after clearance | Required review |
|---|---|---|
| Published articles and book chapters | Versioned, cited retrieval library; selected supervised examples | Exact edition, source rights, applicability and technical accuracy |
| Complete email / work-paper case threads | Structured cases: context → observations → hypotheses → discriminating evidence → outcome | Confidentiality, missing evidence, actual versus inferred outcome |
| Videos, diagrams and teaching notes | Reviewed transcript and annotated explanations | Speech / OCR errors, diagram labels, presenter and recording rights |
| Calculations and rules of thumb | Checked engineering tools and worked examples | Units, assumptions, applicability ranges and independent calculation |
| Quizzes and new expert cases | Training or held-out evaluation, with a recorded split | Keep evaluation answers out of training, retrieval, teacher prompts and caches |

Use the existing expert-capture templates. Each example needs evidence, applicability limits, uncertainties and an accountable technical reviewer. Historical narrative, dialogue and remembered numbers must not become universal operating instructions. Preserve expert disagreement instead of selecting an answer merely because it appears most often.

Group the same plant incident across article, book, video, email and interview as one case family. Keep all derivatives in the same train/development/test partition. Public material may already occur in a base model's pretraining, so it is weak evidence of unseen performance; commission fresh, withheld expert cases for the strongest evaluation.
